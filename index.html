      About

      How can AI systems stay reliable after deployment as their data, environments, and behavior change?

      The second edition of CAO, after ICLR 2026.

      Research themes

      Three connected pillars: notice change, respond, keep watching.

      Catch

      Drift, monitoring, and failure diagnosis
      • Distribution shift, nonstationarity, and drift detection
      • Uncertainty, calibration, and monitoring with limited feedback
      • Behavioral, agent, and tool-use monitoring
      • Failure diagnosis and early-warning signals

      Adapt

      Adaptation, intervention, and recovery
      • Test-time, continual, and online adaptation
      • Safe model updates under changing distributions
      • Abstention, routing, rollback, and recovery
      • Human intervention and escalation

      Operate

      Reliable operation over time
      • Robustness and fairness under evolving conditions
      • Reliability of interactive and autonomous systems
      • Adversarial and system-level failures
      • Auditing, governance, and reliable deployment
      Learn from Failure runs across all three.

      Call for papers

      Work on keeping ML and AI reliable after deployment. Call out November 30; deadline February 1.

      Topics

      • Distribution shift, monitoring, and drift detection
      • Test-time, continual, and online adaptation
      • Uncertainty, calibration, and failure detection
      • Robustness and fairness under changing conditions
      • Reliability of foundation models and agents
      • Safe intervention, recovery, and human escalation
      • Evaluation, benchmarks, and auditing for deployment

      Submission formats

      • Full papers: up to 9 pages in the ICLR format
      • Tiny papers: up to 4 pages

      Non-archival. Double-blind review on OpenReview.

      Learn from Failure track

      Share what didn't work and what you learned. Selected papers seed the From Setbacks to Insights Roundtable after the panel.
