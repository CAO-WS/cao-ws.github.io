About the workshop

Machine learning systems increasingly operate in environments that change after deployment. Data distributions evolve, populations shift, and users change their behavior, so distribution shift remains one of the central challenges in reliable machine learning.

At the same time, foundation models use tools, retrieve information, maintain context, and make sequences of decisions over long periods. Their failures arise not only from distribution shift but also from hallucination, unreliable tool use, compounding errors, adversarial interactions, and unexpected changes in behavior. A system may hallucinate while conventional metrics stay stable. An agent may make individually reasonable decisions whose errors accumulate. An adaptation method may help under a new distribution while damaging behavior that used to be reliable.

How can AI systems remain reliable after deployment as their data, environments, and behavior evolve?

From CAO 2026 to CAO 2027

CAO 2026 asked what happens when the data changes. CAO 2027 asks how we detect and respond when deployed AI changes, or when the world around it changes, and what we learn when that response fails.

The second edition is an expansion of the original agenda, not a departure from it. Distribution shift, drift detection, nonstationarity, test-time adaptation, continual learning, and reliable operation remain core areas.

Two ways the second edition grows

Scientifically, it extends the Catch, Adapt, Operate loop from distribution shift to failures that arise as deployed systems become more interactive and autonomous.

Programmatically, it treats learning from failure as a first-class scientific goal, through a dedicated Lessons from Failures track and an interactive Failure Clinic.

Research themes

The program is organized around three connected pillars. Detection without response gives limited protection, adaptation without reliable monitoring lacks a trustworthy trigger, and any intervention must itself be monitored again.

Catch

Drift, monitoring, and failure diagnosis
  • Distribution shift, nonstationarity, and drift detection
  • Uncertainty, calibration, and monitoring with limited feedback
  • Behavioral, agent, and tool-use monitoring
  • Failure diagnosis and early-warning signals

How can we recognize that a deployed system or its environment has meaningfully changed?

Adapt

Adaptation, intervention, and recovery
  • Test-time, continual, and online adaptation
  • Safe model updates under changing distributions
  • Abstention, routing, rollback, and recovery
  • Human intervention and escalation

When should a deployed system adapt, and when should it abstain, route, recover, or escalate instead?

Operate

Reliable operation over time
  • Robustness and fairness under evolving conditions
  • Reliability of interactive and autonomous systems
  • Adversarial and system-level failures
  • Auditing, governance, and reliable deployment

How can AI systems remain reliable as both the system and its environment continue to evolve?

Lessons from Failures runs across all three pillars: what failed, why it was hard to catch, and what the community should learn from it.

Questions we want to discuss

  • How can distribution shift and other deployment failures be detected when labels are delayed or unavailable?
  • When should detected change trigger adaptation, and when should a system abstain, escalate, or stay unchanged?
  • How can adaptation remain reliable without introducing forgetting, instability, bias, or new vulnerabilities?
  • How should reliability be evaluated when failures emerge over sequences of interactions rather than single predictions?
  • How can monitoring, adaptation, and robustness be designed jointly rather than as separate components?
  • What can the community learn from failed deployments and unsuccessful interventions?

Call for papers

We invite contributions on reliable machine learning and AI after deployment. The full call will open shortly after the workshop is accepted; the details below follow our proposal and may change with the final ICLR 2027 guidelines.

Topics

  • Distribution shift, nonstationarity, monitoring, and drift detection
  • Test-time adaptation, continual learning, and online adaptation
  • Uncertainty, calibration, and failure detection
  • Robustness and fairness under changing conditions
  • Monitoring and reliability of foundation models and agents
  • Safe intervention, recovery, and human escalation
  • Evaluation and benchmarks for deployment reliability
  • Auditing and reliable operation in real-world systems

Work we especially want

Papers that connect more than one stage of the loop: when drift should trigger adaptation, how adaptation changes what monitoring sees next, or how reliability evolves after repeated interventions.

The workshop is non-archival and favors new, exploratory, and in-progress work over finalized publications.

Submission types

Research papers

Original or ongoing work on the CAO themes, in the ICLR format.

Tiny papers

Up to 4 pages: focused ideas, early results, diagnostic studies, new evaluations, and work in progress.

Lessons from Failures papers

Negative findings, deployment postmortems, failed interventions, and careful analyses of unexpected behavior.

Accepted papers will be presented as contributed talks, lightning talks, posters, or in the Failure Clinic.

Lessons from Failures track

Negative results and failed interventions rarely fit conventional publication incentives, so many lessons about deployed systems stay private and get rediscovered. This track welcomes carefully analyzed cases of failed monitoring, unexpected drift, unsuccessful adaptation, surprising behavior, or interventions with unintended consequences. Submissions should answer four questions:

  1. What failed?
  2. Why was the failure hard to detect or address?
  3. What intervention was attempted, and what happened?
  4. What general lesson should the community take away?

Failure Clinic

A few failure cases will be selected for a live session. Each is presented briefly, then diagnosed with researchers from monitoring, distribution shift, adaptation, robustness, and deployment: which signals could have caught it earlier, whether adaptation was the right call, what alternatives existed, and what research questions it raises.

Review process

Double-blind review on OpenReview. We aim for at least three reviews per paper, an average of about two papers per reviewer, and no more than three. Organizers do not handle submissions from their own institutions or recent collaborators, and assignments are screened for institutional, collaboration, and personal conflicts.

Use of LLMs

CAO 2027 follows the official ICLR 2027 policy on LLMs and AI assistants in authoring and reviewing. For tiny papers, AI assistance may be used, but submissions must be primarily human-authored; AI-generated tiny papers will not be accepted. Authors remain responsible for all submitted content.

Taking part

In person, with room for exceptions

CAO 2027 is an in-person workshop, and we strongly encourage speakers, authors, and participants to attend. If visa, accessibility, or unavoidable travel constraints keep you away, and where ICLR permits, an on-site organizer or volunteer can display your poster and relay questions, and you can share a pre-recorded presentation for the website.

Discussion before, during, and after

Subject to ICLR approval, we will run a workshop Discord with spaces for accepted papers, the three pillars, the Failure Clinic, open problems, and introductions. We will also use the discussion features of the official ICLR platform where available. Organizers and student volunteers will moderate and bring questions into the room.

Support for students

We plan to support three student volunteers, covering their registration and giving them hands-on experience alongside the organizing team. CAO 2026 provided ten travel grants; for 2027 we plan to increase financial assistance, prioritizing students, first-time attendees, researchers from under-resourced institutions, underrepresented groups, and anyone facing financial or geographic barriers.

Awards

Best Paper and Best Poster awards recognize especially strong contributed work, including work by junior researchers.

Accessibility

An organizer will serve as accessibility contact and coordinate accommodations with the conference. With author consent and subject to ICLR policy, accepted papers, posters, and talk materials will be shared on this site.
