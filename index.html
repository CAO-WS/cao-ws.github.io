About the workshop

Deployed machine learning systems face data that drift, users who change, and, increasingly, their own unexpected behavior as they use tools and act over long horizons. CAO brings together research on noticing these changes, responding to them, and staying reliable afterwards.

How can AI systems remain reliable after deployment as their data, environments, and behavior evolve?

CAO 2027 builds on the first edition at ICLR 2026. Distribution shift stays at its core; the second edition extends the loop to interactive and autonomous systems and makes learning from failure a first-class topic.

Research themes

The program is organized around three connected pillars. Detection without response gives limited protection, adaptation without reliable monitoring lacks a trustworthy trigger, and any intervention must itself be monitored again.

Catch

Drift, monitoring, and failure diagnosis
  • Distribution shift, nonstationarity, and drift detection
  • Uncertainty, calibration, and monitoring with limited feedback
  • Behavioral, agent, and tool-use monitoring
  • Failure diagnosis and early-warning signals

How can we recognize that a deployed system or its environment has meaningfully changed?

Adapt

Adaptation, intervention, and recovery
  • Test-time, continual, and online adaptation
  • Safe model updates under changing distributions
  • Abstention, routing, rollback, and recovery
  • Human intervention and escalation

When should a deployed system adapt, and when should it abstain, route, recover, or escalate instead?

Operate

Reliable operation over time
  • Robustness and fairness under evolving conditions
  • Reliability of interactive and autonomous systems
  • Adversarial and system-level failures
  • Auditing, governance, and reliable deployment

How can AI systems remain reliable as both the system and its environment continue to evolve?

Lessons from Failures runs across all three pillars: what failed, why it was hard to catch, and what the community should learn from it.

Call for papers

We invite work on keeping machine learning and AI systems reliable after deployment. The full call opens after the workshop is accepted; details may change with the ICLR 2027 guidelines.

Topics

  • Distribution shift, monitoring, and drift detection
  • Test-time, continual, and online adaptation
  • Uncertainty, calibration, and failure detection
  • Robustness and fairness under changing conditions
  • Reliability of foundation models and agents
  • Safe intervention, recovery, and human escalation
  • Evaluation, benchmarks, and auditing for deployment

Work connecting more than one stage of the loop is especially welcome.

Submission types

  • Research papers in the ICLR format
  • Tiny papers of up to 4 pages
  • Lessons from Failures papers

Non-archival. Double-blind review on OpenReview. We follow the ICLR 2027 policy on LLM use; tiny papers must be primarily human-written.

Lessons from Failures and the Failure Clinic

Negative results, failed interventions, and deployment postmortems rarely get published. Tell us what failed, why it was hard to catch, what you tried, and what others should learn. Selected cases will be diagnosed live in the Failure Clinic.

Play the CAO loop

Two one-minute games about keeping deployed models reliable. Nothing is recorded.

Keep it running

You are on call for a deployed model for 60 seconds. The world it serves will change, but you only see a noisy, delayed accuracy trace. Catch real drift, adapt when it matters, and keep serving users.

Time left60 s
Reliability score–
StatusWaiting to start

How to play

  • CCatch: raise an alarm when accuracy really drops. A false alarm costs 3 points.
  • AAdapt: retrain on recent data (needs an alarm first). Every update makes the model forget some of what it knew.
  • SSafe mode: defer users instead of risking errors. No mistakes, but no service either.

      Spot the shift

      Points stream in from a deployed system. At some moment their distribution changes. Press Shift as soon as you notice, without jumping the gun. Then see how you compare with a classic CUSUM drift detector watching the same data.

      How to play

      Five rounds, each with a different kind of change. Press Space, tap the chart, or use the button. Pressing before the change is a false alarm; waiting too long is a miss.
